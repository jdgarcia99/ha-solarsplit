# Changelog

## 0.3.0 (unreleased)

- **Daily objective with a minimum and a deadline** (relay loads, such as the pool pump). The *Daily target* section becomes *Daily objective*: minimum hours (fixed or from an entity), maximum hours used only with surplus (the former target, same keys), day start (00:00 by default, so an objective can count from the evening to the next morning), deadline (fixed, from an `input_datetime`, `time` or timestamp sensor, or the end of the day), *Priority until the minimum*, a preferred window and the hours done sensor. The hours count from the day start.
- **Grid fill at the lowest cost.** New global settings: buy and sell price sensors (attributes `price_00h`…`price_23h`, and `price_next_day_HHh` when published; otherwise today's same hour is the estimate), contracted power and grid fill margin. SolarSplit learns the expected surplus of every hour (median of the last 7 days) and, every hour and when prices change, plans each load whose minimum is missing: hours with enough expected surplus first, then the cheapest grid hours before the deadline (the preferred window first), costing the surplus part of an hour at the sell price and the rest at the buy price. In a planned grid hour the load is switched on without surplus (reason `grid_fill`; `grid_fill_no_price` when there are no prices and the last hours before the deadline are used), with no switch-on delay and respecting its minimum off time. Its power is not counted as surplus. Loads in grid fill stay under the contracted power less the margin, in the plan and live (reason `grid_limit`); with it unknown, one load at a time. In shadow mode the plan is only published.
- The plan ranks every hour before the deadline by its effective cost, sunny or not (a sunny hour costs what its sun would earn exported), the preferred window first: with sun all day it uses the hours when exporting is worth least, not the first ones. *Priority until the minimum* applies only in the hours the plan chose; in any other hour the load keeps its normal priority, so it does not get ahead of other loads to spend sun that is worth more exported.
- The State sensor shows `blocked` only for a load that could not start even with surplus (switched off by something else, a condition off or unknown, maximum reached, out of season, no request). A load off for lack of surplus (`no_budget`, `waiting_higher`, `grid_limit`) is `off`.
- **Solar forecast for the expected surplus.** Optional global *Solar forecast entries* (the Forecast.Solar entries, one per string, or any integration that gives the Energy dashboard a forecast), read through Home Assistant's energy platform and summed. The expected surplus of an hour is the forecast less the house's base load at that hour, learned (median of the last 7 days of PV + import − export − the power of every SolarSplit load, with the PV power sensor) or, before any history, the fixed *Base load* (400 W). Without a forecast, or for a day it does not cover, the surplus seen on the last days. The plan publishes its source (`surplus_source`: `forecast`, `learned` or `none`, and `source` on every hour).
- New per-load sensors for loads with a minimum: *Pending hours*, *Deadline* and *Estimated cost*, with the plan (hours, kWh, prices and cost of every planned hour) as an attribute.
- **Urgency**: optional per-load *Urgency entity* and *Urgency priority*. While the entity is on, the load uses the urgency priority instead of its normal one or the one until the minimum, and wins a tie. For the water heater: `binary_sensor.tankwise_plan_at_risk`, so the sun goes to hot water first when TankWise will not make it. The State sensor shows the `effective_priority`.
- **Saturation of regulable loads.** Until a regulable load is saturated it keeps, in the priority walk, what it could take up to its limit, so a relay load behind it does not start with that surplus. It is saturated after a minute with its new optional *Output entity* (%, the diverter's duty) at 99 % or more, or with surplus still exported while it draws well below its limit (a thermostat cut); then the loads behind it get what it does not use. State attribute `saturated`.

## 0.2.0 (unreleased)

- **Power learned from the circuit**: optional per-load *Circuit sensor* (the power of a circuit shared with other loads, such as an IoTaWatt channel). Each switch of the load, by SolarSplit or anyone else, measures the circuit's jump (mean of the 20 s before against 20–60 s after, skipping a compressor's start); a measurement is discarded if another load of the circuit switched in the window, or if the jump is below 50 W or above twice the nominal power. The last 10 valid measurements are kept in the store; from 3 on their mean replaces the nominal power, unless the load has its own power sensor. A climate load that is on counts at least its share of the circuit. New sensor *Learned power* (W) per load with a circuit sensor, with the number of measurements, the last one and whether it is in use.

- Translations of the attributes of *Last decision* (`load`, `reason` with every reason code, `budget`, `shadow`) and of each load's *State* (`power`, `limit`), in English and Spanish. A test checks that `strings.json`, `en.json` and `es.json` have the same keys and that every enum state, selector option, reason code, form field and entity has its text.

- Fix: the load form's sections came with an empty default, so the frontend sent them empty: their fields showed blank and editing a load cleared its optional entities (a regulable load could not be saved again). Sections are now required and without a default, like in Home Assistant core; the frontend fills them from their fields.
- Loads **only on demand** (*Manual control and season* section): a load switched on by anything but SolarSplit starts a request, and switched off by anything but SolarSplit ends it. With a request it is managed by priority (switched off without surplus, after its delays and minimum on time, and on again when the surplus returns; that switch-off keeps the request); without one it is never switched on (reason `waiting_request`) and its power is not reassigned. A climate gets back its HVAC mode, target temperature and fan mode from the request. Replaces *Respect manual changes* for that load. New binary sensor *Request active*; the request is kept in the store.
- **Manage again automatically**: with Manage switched off, it switches back on by itself when the load goes off or at a configurable local time (00:00 by default). With Manage off the load's power is not reassigned.
- **Season**: optional global *Season entity* (`input_select` or `select`) and per-load *Seasons* (its options; empty = all year). Out of season a load is not used (reason `out_of_season`) and, if SolarSplit switched it on, it is switched off without the switch-off delay, after its minimum on time; a regulable load gets its minimum. Combined with the usage condition: both must hold. An unusable season entity leaves the load as it is (`season_unknown`).

- Optional signed grid power sensor, instead of the export and import sensors, with a sign setting (export negative, the default, or positive). With one sensor, a reading not reported within the stale data timeout is unknown, whatever it reads; separate sensors keep their rule. The forms require one mode or the other.
- After a restart, before the first cycle, a regulable load outside its automatic mode whose limit SolarSplit had lowered (according to the saved memory) gets its maximum back, so a restart does not leave a manual heater boost held back. With unknown grid readings the maximum is also given back to regulable loads outside automatic mode or not managed. A limit already at its maximum is not written again.
- Shadow mode never writes a regulable load's limit, not to lower it nor to give it back: the check sits in the one function that writes it. A test covers every path (cycle, switches, unload, start).
- Docs: the water heater is a regulable load behind the x10derivador (FreeDS is gone), with its configuration in the README; its safety cut (the Shelly) is independent of SolarSplit and is not implemented in it.
- Daily target per load, in a collapsible *Daily target* section: hours on today sensor (or SolarSplit's own count), target hours from an entity (`input_number`, `number` or `sensor`) or a fixed value. Once reached, the load is not eligible until the next day (reason `target_reached`) and is switched off after its minimum on time.
- New per-load sensor *Time on today* (h): SolarSplit's own count of the hours the load was on today, reset at local midnight, kept across restarts.
- New load type **Regulable** (for example a water heater behind a diverter), configured in a collapsible *Regulable load* section of the load form: power sensor, limit entity, mode entity and its automatic value, minimum and maximum limit, smallest change written (100 W) and minimum time between writes (30 s). In automatic mode its power counts in the budget and SolarSplit writes its limit: the maximum when nothing ahead of it uses the budget, otherwise what the loads ahead leave (reasons `full_power`, `limited`). In any other mode it is `external` and its power is grid power. SolarSplit never switches it; with its switch off it is blocked. The maximum is written back when SolarSplit stops limiting it (master switch, Manage, other mode, global condition, unload). The State sensor shows the limit as an attribute.
- Unknown data is never 0 W. A missing or frozen grid reading makes the cycle decide nothing and publish the reason `grid_unknown` (the budget becomes unknown; entities stay available). New setting *Stale data timeout* (20 s): a non-zero grid reading not reported for longer, or both grid sensors quiet at once, counts as unknown.
- An unknown zero-injection entity pauses the loop (`mode_unknown`); an unknown global or usage condition leaves its loads as they are (`global_unknown`, `condition_unknown`) instead of switching them off. A variable load whose power or regulation entity cannot be read is unavailable.
- A load switched off by something else (a safety cut, a person, an automation) is `blocked` with reason `switched_off` and SolarSplit never switches it back on, even without *Respect manual changes*, until it is on again or its Manage switch is turned off and on. Not detected in shadow mode.
- New brand: icon and README banner, with SVG sources in `images/`. The orange logos and `scripts/render_brand.py` are gone.

## 0.1.0 (unreleased)

- Loads as config subentries: add, edit and delete each load from the UI.
- Control loop (coordinator): reads the grid sensors, smooths them, builds every load's state, stores the engine's delay marks and, outside shadow mode, switches the chosen load (`switch`, `input_boolean` or `climate`).
- Shadow mode setting, on by default: the loop decides and publishes without switching anything.
- Entities: master switch, available budget, assigned power, mode and last decision; per load, a managed switch, state and reason sensors and a priority number.
- Manual changes: a load switched by someone else is left alone until it returns to the state SolarSplit set, or its Manage switch is turned on again.
- Diagnostics with the settings, the loads and the last cycle.
- Persistent memory (Home Assistant store): what SolarSplit switched, each load's last transition time, delay marks (dropped after a gap of more than 2 minutes), the master and Manage switches. After a reload or restart a load switched on by SolarSplit stays managed; one changed while Home Assistant was stopped becomes manual. Minimum on/off times use SolarSplit's own transition time, which survives restarts.
- Priority changes from the priority entity apply on the fly without reloading, and are stored in the load's subentry.
- Global condition off: loads switched on by SolarSplit that respect the condition are switched off (reason `global_shutdown`, no off delay, minimum on time respected). Manual loads, loads that ignore the condition and the master switch behave as before. In shadow mode the decision is only published.
- Every entity gets the label "Gestión de excedentes", created if missing.
- README: step-by-step install from `main` through a HACS custom repository and load setup, to try it in shadow mode.
- UI descriptions of the global condition and *Respect the global condition* explain the switch-off.
- Validate workflow runs ruff over the whole repository.
- Engine: unavailable loads and loads held by the global condition are left as they are, and their power is not reassigned.
- Requires Home Assistant 2025.6 or later.

## 0.0.2

- Engine: per-load switch-on and switch-off delays. The engine returns, per load, since when each condition holds (`on_since`, `off_since`); the caller stores the marks and passes them back. A delay not yet met yields the `on_delay` / `off_delay` reason.
- Brand assets (icon and logos) for the HACS brands check, rendered by `scripts/render_brand.py`.
- Validate workflow also runs ruff.
